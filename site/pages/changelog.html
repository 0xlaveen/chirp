October 27, 2026

Re-transcribe kept audio on Mac and Android

Choose Re-transcribe… on a stopped note with kept audio. Chirp asks every time, shows the audio length and destination, and sends clips only to your own Chirp server. The transcript keeps speaker names and turn assignments; your notes body stays unless you separately choose to rebuild it. Undo and Restore previous transcript recover the previous version. Debug retention gets an explicit warning. iOS re-transcription is in progress.

Longer Mac dictation, learning on iOS and a choice of stopping

Mac dictation now rolls across segments for about 10 minutes and adds Spoken commands for a trailing “new line” or “new paragraph”. Mac and Android have a temporary Private session, ending on lock, after one hour or app exit. On iOS the persistent switch is named Pause dictation history. iPhone and iPad now offer Learn from my edits, Teach Chirp and Learned, with keyboard vocabulary learning and no per-app styles. They also gain Stop dictation → When I pause, alongside manual Stop.

Blank typed notes and precise speaker fixes

Android’s New note opens a blank document without a microphone or a paired computer, alongside Record. Mac already offers New Note in its Notes window. On Mac and Android, This turn is… corrects one sentence’s speaker. Both a direct blank-note action and single-turn reassignment are still in progress on iOS.

#284 · Android New note and speaker turns

Notes in iOS Shortcuts and Control Center

Record, Pause/Resume and Stop note actions are available in Shortcuts and as Control Center controls; starting a note opens Chirp. The Keyboard shortcuts page also lists the Notes bindings.

#289 · Notes system actions and shortcuts

Speaker labels are on by default, with Saved voices on every device

Label speakers is now on by default on the Mac, Android and iPhone/iPad, and the speaker model downloads by itself the first time, with a status line and a Turn off, instead of asking first. Naming a person can remember their voice in one Saved voices library on your Chirp server, shared by every device that uses it: name someone on your phone and your Mac recognizes them too. A Name speakers sheet appears after Stop on all three. Android and iOS ask your computer to compare voices; the Mac still labels on the Mac itself.

iPhone and iPad: a keyboard top row and a hardware-keyboard shortcut

Chirp Keyboard’s top row now matches Android’s: word suggestions in the middle, options and the mic at the edges, and the same height in every state. On iPad, hold Right ⌥ Option inside Chirp to dictate. Elsewhere iPadOS only allows a toggle: make a Shortcuts key with Full Keyboard Access to start and stop.

“Your computer”, not “your Mac”

The Android and iOS apps now say “your computer” where they used to say “your Mac”, since your Chirp server can be a Mac, a Windows PC or a Linux box.

October 26, 2026

Chirp comes to Windows and Linux

The Chirp server now runs on Windows 10/11 and Linux, with a background service on each (a per-user Task Scheduler task on Windows, a systemd user unit on Linux), ONNX Parakeet on the CPU with opt-in CUDA/DirectML, and a shared Tauri desktop app: tray icon, hold-to-talk key, live HUD, typed or pasted insertion and a local Notes library with owner-only file permissions. On Linux, X11 gets single-key holds and Wayland uses the desktop’s Global Shortcuts portal. It’s early: Windows is verified in CI, with a manual acceptance pass on real PCs still pending, and CPU clean-up without a GPU is slow.
