Use Chirp on Android

Chirp for Android is a notes app and a way to dictate in any app. Use its own keyboard, Chirp Keyboard, or keep Gboard or Samsung Keyboard and use a small floating mic. Either way your voice goes to the Chirp server on your computer, and the text comes back. There’s no hosted APK yet — a packaged Android app is coming soon.

Before you start

No hosted APK yet.

A packaged Android app is coming soon. Until then this guide builds and signs the APK on your computer. New here? See what’s available today, or use the Android agent prompt.

  • Chirp is working on your computer — see the Mac, Windows or Linux setup guide. The computer needs to be awake when you dictate from the phone.
  • An Android phone and a USB cable (or wireless debugging). Chirp is tuned for the Galaxy Z Fold but works as a regular keyboard elsewhere.

Connect your phone and computer with Tailscale

Your phone reaches the computer through Tailscale, a private network between your own devices (a “tailnet”). Nothing is exposed to the public internet.

  1. Install Tailscale on both

    Install it on the computer and the phone and sign in with the same account. Both should show as connected.

  2. Allow HTTPS on your tailnet

    Chirp publishes the server on your computer to your tailnet with Tailscale Serve, which needs MagicDNS and HTTPS certificates turned on (admin console → DNS). If they're off, Tailscale tells you when you pair, with a link to fix it.

Build the app

On the computer, in the chirp folder from the setup guide. The first command installs a Java and Android toolchain just for your user — no admin password, no emulator images:

android/scripts/setup-toolchain.sh
source ~/.local/share/chirp-android/env.sh
android/scripts/make-keystore.sh
cd android
./gradlew assembleRelease

make-keystore.sh creates your signing key once. Keep it: later updates must be signed with the same key, or Android will refuse them.

The phone's app ends up at android/app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-arm64-v8a-release.apk.

Install it on the phone

Pick one:

  • Taildrop (no cable): tailscale file cp app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-arm64-v8a-release.apk <your-phone>: — then tap the file in the phone's Tailscale notification.
  • USB: turn on USB debugging in the phone's Developer options, plug it in and run adb install -r app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-arm64-v8a-release.apk.
Play Protect may warn you.

It flags any app that doesn't come from the Play Store. Choose More details → Install anyway.

Open Chirp. Onboarding is five short pages, with one button at the bottom of each:

  1. Welcome

    Dictation and notes, in one picture.

  2. How you’ll dictate

    Choose Use Chirp Keyboard to switch to Chirp’s keyboard, or Keep Gboard (it shows your keyboard’s name) to keep yours and use the floating mic. Notes and the Scratchpad work either way, and you can change your mind later in Settings → How you dictate.

  3. Turn on the keyboard, or the floating mic

    For Chirp Keyboard: enable it, then select it. On Samsung: Settings → General management → Keyboard list and default. For the floating mic, see Keep your keyboard.

  4. Allow the microphone

    It's only open while you dictate or record a note.

  5. Pair your computer

    See the next section.

Pair with your computer

  1. Show the pairing code on the computer

    In Chirp for Mac open Settings → Devices → Pair a Phone… If your tailnet isn't serving Chirp yet, it shows the exact Tailscale command and runs it only after you confirm. Then it shows a QR code. On Windows or Linux, uv run chirp-server pair prints the same QR (see those guides).

  2. Scan it on the phone

    On the last onboarding page tap Scan code. Chirp tests the connection and saves the pairing by itself; nothing is saved until the connection works. Then tap Finish and Chirp opens on Notes, with a chip at the top showing it’s connected to your computer.

The QR code is a secret.

It contains the key to your computer's speech server. Chirp for Mac hides the window from screenshots and screen sharing and closes it after two minutes. Don't post it anywhere.

Dictate with Chirp Keyboard

  1. Tap the mic

    It's in the row above the keys. Your words appear underlined in the field as you speak.

  2. Tap stop to insert

    The final text replaces the underlined preview. ✕ throws it away.

  3. Undo or Use raw

    For five seconds afterwards, Undo removes exactly what was inserted, and Use raw swaps clean-up's version for what you actually said.

  • Long-press the mic to turn clean-up on or off and choose a style.
  • Stop when I pause: Settings → Dictation → Stopping → When I pause.
  • Long dictations work up to 10 minutes; Chirp splits them quietly so nothing is dropped.
  • Lost connection? What was heard so far is kept, never thrown away.
  • History is on by default: your recent dictations are kept, encrypted, on the phone only, for 7 days (24 hours or 30 days are also available). Turn it off in Settings → Privacy & history, which deletes what is kept.

Keep your keyboard

Chose Keep Gboard? Chirp sets up its floating mic: a small bubble that appears at the edge of the screen when a text field you can dictate into has focus.

  1. Read what it can see

    The floating mic works through Android Accessibility. Chirp explains first: it reads only the focused text field, on the phone, so it can put your words in. Password fields are skipped.

  2. Turn it on

    Chirp opens the Chirp floating mic page in Android’s Accessibility settings; switch it on. If Android says Restricted setting (it does for apps you installed yourself), come back to Chirp and follow Did Android say “Restricted setting”?: app info → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings, then try again.

  3. Dictate

    Tap the bubble to dictate, or hold it to talk and let go to stop. Drag it anywhere; it snaps to the nearer edge.

  • If an app won’t take the text, your words wait in the panel with Copy and Open in Scratchpad. Chirp never copies on its own, and the words are cleared after 2 minutes.
  • It doesn’t work in every app, and it doesn’t change what Gboard’s or Samsung’s own mic button does.
  • The bubble hides while Chirp Keyboard is the active keyboard, since that has its own mic.

More ways into Chirp

The Dictate Quick Settings tile and the home-screen widget open Quick Dictate, which copies the finished text for you to paste. Share selected text from another app into Notes. Apps that launch Android’s speech-input activity can offer Chirp as a handler. This does not replace the mic inside Gboard or Samsung Keyboard.

Scratchpad and Notes

Chirp opens on Notes. Tap Record for a meeting or a long think-aloud: Notes on Android.

The small mic button beside Record opens the Scratchpad, a blank page for dictating longer text outside any app. (You can also long-press the Chirp icon, or add the Scratchpad tile to Quick Settings.) Dictate, edit, then Copy or Share, or keep it with Save as note. Otherwise the text is gone when you leave.

If something's off

  • “Tailscale is off” in the keyboard: tap Turn on, or open the Tailscale app.
  • Computer unreachable: is the computer awake, with Chirp running and Tailscale connected? The connection chip at the top of Notes opens Settings → Connection, where Check connection tests it.
  • Updates: install new builds over the old one — don't uninstall first, or you'll lose your pairing.
