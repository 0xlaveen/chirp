Local-first voice dictation

Talk anywhere.
Type nowhere.

Chirp is voice dictation for your Mac, Android and iPhone. Speech recognition runs on your own Mac, and nothing is retained. Press a key, talk, and the words land in whatever app you are using.

How it works

Your Mac does the listening

One speech server runs on your Mac. The apps are thin: they capture audio, stream it, and insert the text that comes back. Phones reach the Mac only through your own private Tailscale network.

  1. You talk

    Press the hotkey on your Mac, or tap the mic on your phone's keyboard, in any app.

  2. Your Mac listens

    Parakeet runs on your Mac's Apple Silicon and streams live words back while you speak.

  3. Text lands

    The final text replaces the live preview at your cursor. Optional clean-up removes the “um”s first.

Why Chirp

Your voice stays on hardware you own

Chirp is for people who dictate a lot and would rather their speech never left home. Here is the honest trade-off.

What is keptNothing by default. History is opt-in and encrypted on the phoneDepends on the provider's policyDepends on the vendor's policy and your settings

Clean-upOptional local model, styles per app, raw text one keystroke awayOften, on their serversVaries; mostly punctuation
Meetings and long notesLive Notes: Markdown notes while you talk, on the Mac or an Android phoneUsually a separate productNot built in
Cost and accountNo subscription, no account. Open models: Parakeet for speech, optional Qwen clean-upUsually a subscription and an accountIncluded with the OS

The limits, plainly: Chirp needs an Apple Silicon Mac that's switched on. Phones connect only through Tailscale. Everything installs from source — there are no notarized, Play Store or App Store builds — and the iPhone app has passed simulator tests but hasn't run on a device yet.

Privacy

Private by default, not by setting

Chirp keeps audio and text in memory only for as long as one dictation takes. These are the defaults, and the code is open for you to check.

Nothing retained

No recordings, transcripts or logs of what you said. Logs carry only timings, byte counts and error codes. No telemetry. The one exception you can see: unsaved Mac Live Notes keep a text-only recovery copy until you save or discard them.

Recognition on your Mac

The server listens on 127.0.0.1 only. Clean-up runs as a local model too, unless you deliberately point it elsewhere — and then the apps say so.

Tailnet only

Phones reach the Mac through Tailscale Serve on your private tailnet. Chirp refuses to pair while Tailscale Funnel is on.

Secret pairing

Pairing uses a bearer token shown as a QR code on your Mac. Phones store it encrypted; the Mac's pairing window is hidden from screen sharing.

Opt-in history

Phone history is off until you turn it on, then encrypted on the device and excluded from backups.

Loud debugging

Keeping recordings for debugging is opt-in, local, expires after three days, and shows a red warning in every app.
