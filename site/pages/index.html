Private dictation and live notes

Talk anywhere.
Your Mac writes it down.

Chirp turns your voice into text in any app, and long conversations into notes you can search and replay. Your own Mac is the engine: speech recognition and clean-up run on it, your phone reaches it over your private network, and there is no cloud, no account and no telemetry.

Dictate anywhere

Press a key, talk, and the words land where you’re typing

Live words appear as you speak. When you stop, the final text replaces them about a tenth of a second later, in Mail, Slack, a terminal or a form field.

  • ⌃⌥Space on the Mac, or any shortcut you record (even ⌥ alone). Auto-stop when you pause, or hold to talk.
  • Optional clean-up on your Mac removes the “um”s and fixes punctuation, with chat, email, code and notes styles picked per app. Use raw puts back exactly what you said.
  • On Android, use Chirp Keyboard (a full keyboard with a mic key) or keep your own keyboard and use the floating mic.
  • Nothing you say is lost. If a dictation fails part-way, Chirp keeps what it heard.

Notes that remember

Leave the mic open. Get notes you can keep.

Press ⌃⌥N on the Mac, or tap Record on Android, and talk through a meeting or a long think-aloud. Chirp writes headings, to-dos, decisions and questions while you speak, next to the full transcript. Every note saves itself into a library on the device that recorded it. There is no Save step.

A library, not a pile of files

Notes are listed newest first, grouped by day, with open to-dos counted. They’re kept until you delete them, and a deleted note waits 30 days in Recently Deleted. Pick up a finished note any time with Continue.

Find anything

Search covers titles, notes and transcripts, with the matches highlighted. A note that was interrupted by a crash comes back marked Recovered, not lost.

Replay what was said

Turn on Keep audio with notes and each spoken segment is kept with its note. Press play beside a transcript paragraph to hear it again. It’s off by default, and the audio never leaves the device.

Keep your keyboard

Love Gboard or Samsung Keyboard? Keep it.

When you set Chirp up on Android, choose how you’ll dictate. Keep my keyboard keeps the keyboard you already use and adds a small Chirp mic beside text fields when you need it.

  • Tap the bubble to dictate, or hold it to talk. Drag it, and it snaps to the nearer edge.
  • It stays out of the way: no bubble in password fields, and none while Chirp Keyboard is in use.
  • Your words never disappear. If an app won’t take the text, it waits in the panel with Copy and Open in Scratchpad. Chirp never copies on its own.
  • Plainly: it doesn’t work in every app, and it doesn’t change what Gboard’s or Samsung’s own mic button does. Notes and the Scratchpad work either way.

Privacy

Your Mac is the engine. There is no cloud.

Chirp has no servers of its own, no account and no telemetry. Audio and text only move between your own devices, over your own private Tailscale network.

Recognised on your Mac

Speech recognition and clean-up run on your Mac. Its server listens only on the Mac itself, and clean-up stays local unless you deliberately point it elsewhere, in which case every app says where text goes.

Only your devices

Phones reach the Mac through Tailscale on your own tailnet, never the public internet. Each phone pairs with a secret QR code and its own token, which you can revoke.

Notes stay put

Notes live on the device that recorded them, kept until you delete them (or for 1 year, 90 or 30 days if you choose). They’re never synced or indexed. On Android they’re encrypted with a key held by the phone.

Audio only if you ask

Dictation audio is gone when each dictation ends. Keeping audio with notes is off by default, and so is dictation history. Logs hold timings and error codes, never what you said.

A debug mode that keeps recordings exists for troubleshooting. It’s off unless you start it on the Mac, and every app shows a red warning while it’s on. Everything Chirp keeps, and where: docs/privacy.md.
